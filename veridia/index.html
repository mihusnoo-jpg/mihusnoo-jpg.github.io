The Covenant of the People

The old world is gone. Survivors from five continents, strangers with nothing in common but loss, are drawn toward a single valley by a signal no one can explain.

What they build there begins with a refusal: no rebuilt hierarchy, no hidden rooms. Their founding document is written in radical transparency, with the help of an intelligence engineered to notice what power tries to conceal, and structurally forbidden from ever ruling on what happens next. A partner with eyes and no hands.

Veridia was written as fiction. It may not be read only as that.

The harder question

What if the wars and divisions that broke the old world were never inevitable, but manufactured by people who profited from them and dressed up as democracy? Veridia asks what becomes possible once that lie is seen clearly.

The book

This is post-apocalyptic fiction that refuses both nihilism and nostalgia. It closes a century later, on what any of us leave behind: the good and the harm, in equal and undiminishing weight.
