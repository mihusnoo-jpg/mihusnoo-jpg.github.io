Meet Me

My Work, a Lifetime Passion

I began my career in 1987 and followed my passion for design as a freelance graphic designer. Since then, I’ve spent the better part of the last three decades working successfully in the design, media and industrial businesses.

I’ve been around the world seeking more knowledge, learning and sharing the use of several CNC equipment types in the manufacturing sector. My extensive hands-on experience and my devotion to perfection allowed me to leap into different leading roles.

My positive and enthusiastic behaviour has enabled me to communicate effectively at all levels. This career reflects a total involvement of my high motivation, leadership qualities, persuasive interaction and communication with the people around me.

On numerous occasions I was commended for the consistency of my performance, my attention to detail, and my ability to solve and prevent many types of issues. I am very attentive to exact specifications as well as cost, quality and time objectives.

Recruiting and training people has been a constant thread through my career. I haven’t kept a precise count, but it runs to more than 2,000 across the years — most of them in furniture manufacturing, and some in the sign industry in Mauritius, where I managed the implementation and training of a team of young, fresh recruits to set up a new sign factory of more than 75 personnel.

I believe that while I’m living a passionate life, I have achieved and applied good practices all the way, and will always strive to keep up the good work for that great feeling of satisfaction.

Since 2017 — a new chapter in Indonesia

My work took me to Indonesia — the country where much of the world’s furniture is made. I took over a furniture business that was drowning and guided it back to profit: sorting out how the work was organised, how the money was tracked, and how the factory and the showroom talked to each other.

Once the business was healthy, the next weak link was the paperwork itself — orders, production, quality, shipping and wages living in spreadsheets, paper and memory. So I designed and built Veridian, a complete ERP written from scratch, 100% fitted to how we work, to make the business even more efficient.

From rescue to group

Before Indonesia, I spent several years as maintenance consultant for all McDonald’s restaurants in Mauritius. It taught me scale and standards — and it showed me, up close, how much of a business depends on the people at the bottom of the ladder being treated well. That conviction is part of why I later chose to build companies rather than advise them.

Then a six-month consulting assignment brought me back to Indonesia to rescue a company. The situation was far worse than anyone had described, so I stayed. Before long I was asked to take over its direction — and since then we’ve grown into two sister companies in Indonesia and another in Tanzania.

Veridian grew with us. What began as an ERP for a single factory now runs the group — still written from scratch, still fitted to how we actually work, and still the backbone of how orders, production, quality, shipping and wages are tracked across countries.
